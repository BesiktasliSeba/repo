MacStatusBar&Dock

A MacBook-style desktop for your jailbroken iPad.

A menu bar with real app menus, Mac windows, a magnifying Dock and much more. It works with just your fingers or with a keyboard and trackpad or mouse, in every orientation. Optional extras, like the auto-hiding menu bar, start switched off, and most features have their own switch in Settings.

Features

Menu bar

A real Mac menu bar: an Apple menu (About This iPad, App Store, Force Quit, Respring, Safe Mode, Lock Screen, Sleep, Shut Down) and menus for the app in front (App, Edit, Go, Window), in every orientation, mirrored in right-to-left languages as on a Mac. Tap the clock for a Today drop-down with your widgets and notifications. Audio mixing per app, with a volume for each app. A VPN menu with Disconnect, and a Wi-Fi menu to switch networks without Control Center. The battery opens a battery menu with Low Power Mode and Sleep Guard, and a Control Center icon next to the clock opens Control Center. Control Center Style in Settings > Status Bar picks a Mac Control Center (Airplane Mode, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Display, Sound and Now Playing above your other controls), iPadOS's own or BigSurCenter, and only that one loads. Status Bar Style switches between Mac and the stock iPadOS bar. Menu Bar Size in Settings > Status Bar: Medium and Large make the menu bar taller, with larger text, icons and menus that are easier to tap with a finger. Live Activities in Menu Bar in Settings > Status Bar shows what's playing and your calls as small pills next to the status icons, as on a Mac. A tap on one opens a card to control it. On iPadOS 15.6.1, 16.7.7, 17.5.1 and 18.5, Clock timers show there too. Battery Percentage in Icon in Settings > Status Bar shows the battery level as a number inside the battery icon.

Windows

Mac windows on the engine you already use (Aerial 3.0, 5.0 or 5.1, Zetsu 1.6.2 or 1.6.6, MilkyWay4 on iPadOS 15): title bars, traffic lights, rounded corners, resize handles and Fit to Window tiling. Or Stage Manager as the window engine (iPadOS 16 to 18): Apple's own windowing with the Mac look, over your Home Screen and desktop. Windows stay where you put them and at the size you give them, and an app can have more than one window. Going Home minimizes them so the Home Screen shows, and opening an app brings its window back. Only the window you're using keeps a text cursor, and Esc ends typing. Windows have a smallest size with every engine, as in Stage Manager, and new windows open at the Window Size you pick: Small, Default or Large. With Aerial, Zetsu or MilkyWay4 on iPadOS 15 to 18, every app with a window also gets its App Switcher card. A window you move or resize lines up with the screen edges, the menu bar, the Dock and other windows when you let go of it near them. When you turn the iPad, a window in a layout (Fill Screen, a half or a quarter) takes the same layout in the new orientation, and any other window goes back to where it last was in that orientation. New Windows Open at Fill and Double-Tap a Title Bar to (Fill, Zoom or Minimize) in Settings > Windows & Multitasking work as on a Mac, and Window Tiling Shortcuts in Settings > Keyboard turns on macOS 15's Globe-Control keys for tiling. Keep Covered Windows in Front in Settings > Windows & Multitasking: when a window comes forward, a smaller window it would cover completely stays in front of it. It works with Aerial, Zetsu and MilkyWay4, and its row isn't shown with Stage Manager. KeyPilot by wanghuangjie is the companion tweak for shortcuts of your own, app launching and URL actions, and its window layouts use MacStatusBar&Dock's windows. Settings > Keyboard has a row to get or open it.

Mac Switcher

Desktops and Mission Control like on a Mac, in place of the App Switcher (Settings > Windows & Multitasking > Mac Switcher, off until you switch it on). Swipe up and hold, double press the Home button or press Control-Up on a keyboard to see your desktops and windows. Tap + to add a desktop (up to 8), swipe sideways with four fingers or along the bottom edge and the desktops slide with your fingers, and drag a window onto another desktop to move it. Desktops work with every window engine.

Finder

A Mac Finder window on every engine: sidebar, list and icon views, Quick Look, Search, Undo and Put Back, several-item selection, drag and drop into apps, and Share, which hands an app over as an .ipa. On My iPad shows the same folders as the Files app, and the Trash has an Empty button. Home Screen folders you pick are listed in the menu bar's Finder menu. A desktop on the first Home Screen page for your files and folders, with their menus over your windows, names you can hide, an icon size and app icon corners you pick in Settings > Desktop, and a small TextEdit window for text files. Finder shows folder sizes in its list and in Get Info, worked out in the background. Click a column header in Finder's list to sort by Name, Date Modified, Size or Kind, and click it again to reverse the order. You can also sort from the View menu. Quick Look also previews Office files and CSV, and you can move between previews with the arrow keys or a swipe. On the desktop, with an item selected, the arrow keys move the selection and Space opens Quick Look, as in Finder. It only changes files in your own places, so it can't be used to break the iPad by accident.

Drives in Finder

USB sticks, SSDs and SD card readers show up under Locations as soon as you connect them. On iPadOS 16 to 18, each drive has an Eject button in Finder's sidebar, as on a Mac, and File > Eject and the drive's menu eject it too. exFAT and FAT32 work (FAT32 holds no files over 4 GB); APFS and Mac OS Extended should work too but are not tested yet; NTFS is not supported by iPadOS. SSDs may need more power: connect them straight to the iPad's USB-C port or use a powered hub.

Dock

A macOS Dock: magnification, recent apps, dots under open apps, Finder and Launchpad at its start like on a Mac, and a Downloads stack with search that you can drag files into and out of. In landscape it can sit on the left or right instead, in Settings > Dock > Position on Screen. Settings > Dock also has Dock Style (Default, Glass Shelf or Frosted, the Docks of older macOS), Automatically Hide and Show the Dock, and Animate Opening Applications, which bounces an app's icon while it opens. A Trash at its end, off until you turn it on in Settings > Dock: drop files on it to move them to the Trash, a Dock app to take it out of the Dock, or a drive to eject it (iPadOS 16 to 18). Menus that open from the Dock and the Downloads stack sit over their icon with the Mac's arrow pointing at it. On iPadOS 15 to 17, icon themes such as SnowBoard's change Finder, Launchpad, Downloads, the Trash and Activity Monitor like any app icon. Theme makers find the identifiers in the README on GitHub. In Edit Home Screen, Finder, Launchpad, Downloads and the Trash jiggle with the other Dock icons and don't open when tapped. On iPadOS 15 to 17, SnowBoard's double-tap menu works on them too.

Activity Monitor

A Mac-style Activity Monitor window with the running processes and their CPU, memory and disk use: sort, search, inspect a process and quit apps. Open it from the Go menu, the RAM & CPU panel in Control Center or Spotlight's actions. System processes show no figures, and the Energy and Network tabs have none per process. It's also an app in the App Library, in Utilities and in its search. Add to Dock in its menu puts it in the Dock, where it works like an app. While its window is open, also minimized, it shows in the Dock as an open app, with a dot, and leaves when the window closes. Keep in Dock in its menu keeps it there. While its window is open, it's also in Command-Tab with your apps, in the order you last used it.

Built to be safe

Automatic Crash Recovery: if a feature is followed by two SpringBoard crashes in a row, it switches itself off instead of leaving you in a crash loop, tells you in Settings and offers Report a Problem; another tweak's crash never switches MacStatusBar&Dock off. Off by default on untested iPadOS versions (unless you choose Enable Anyway), does nothing on an iPhone, and never contacts any server.

Also included

A Mac pointer (arrow and I-beam, with Pointer Control colors as an option), Mac-style notification banners (or iPadOS's or Destra's, with Banner Style), Mac-style Haptic Touch menus (Force Quit, App Size and Add App to Folder on app icons, folder shortcuts), a mute icon, Control Center from the menu bar, RAM & CPU and SSH controls for Control Center, a customizable Go menu, an auto-hiding menu bar, Home Screen options (page dots, icon labels, Home Bar, and whether a swipe past the last page opens the App Library), Lock Screen options, keyboard extras (Cmd-Tab to the right window, Esc closes menus, Tab to mute, Globe volume and brightness keys), longer Auto-Lock times, Battery Health in Settings > Battery with Sleep Guard, Keep Tailspin Off to save memory, a search field in the Settings sidebar, Spotlight extras you can turn on (tweak settings, actions, your windows, your Desktop and Downloads files), an SSH switch, Ethernet settings and Reduce Motion support.

The Mac look is on right after install, and most features have their own switch in Settings > Status Bar, Desktop, Windows & Multitasking and Dock. Some extras start off, such as the Mac Switcher, the Dock's Trash, the auto-hiding menu bar, seconds in the clock, the keyboard extras and the Lock Screen options. Apps open full screen until you tap the green light or turn on Window > Open Apps as Windows (with the Stage Manager engine they open as windows from the start).

Which window engine?

On iPadOS 16.1 to 18, an iPad with Stage Manager (or an older iPad with TrollPad) starts with Stage Manager, Apple's own windowing with the Mac look, tested on iPadOS 16.7.7, 17.5.1 and 18.5. Anywhere else, start with Aerial. Zetsu works well too and is a good alternative. MilkyWay4 runs on iPadOS 15 only. Updating keeps the engine you picked.

Report a Problem

The Report a Problem button (Settings > Status Bar, and the Automatic Crash Recovery note) opens a new GitHub issue in your browser, filled in with your iPad model, iPadOS version, window engine and its version, and the tweak's version. If the crash protection switched something off in the last 7 days, it adds what it turned off and when, and a few lines naming the tweak's own code that crashed (nothing from other apps or tweaks); on an untested iPadOS version, which of the tweak's switches are on. The report is part of the link to GitHub, so GitHub receives its text when the page opens. You see the whole text there, can change or delete any of it, and it's only posted as an issue if you submit it (a GitHub account is needed). Nothing personal is included, and the tweak collects nothing in the background.

Known limitations

  • The tweak's own menus and Settings pages are in English only.
  • Apple's Stage Manager (iPadOS 16 to 18) stays off while another window engine runs, because two window systems would fight over the same apps. To use it, pick Stage Manager as the window engine in Settings > Windows & Multitasking > Window Engine.
  • With the Stage Manager engine, a desktop holds up to 7 windows.
  • Some apps, like many games, only run full screen. The camera works only full screen (iPadOS pauses it in a window), and Photos opened from the Camera stays full screen.
  • Finder can't show iCloud Drive (iPadOS doesn't allow it), drag and drop goes from Finder into apps but not back yet, and drives have been tested on iPadOS 15 only.
  • On iPadOS 15, Safari's sound can stop another app that plays alone, as on a stock iPad. Control Center's Now Playing shows one app at a time.
  • On iPadOS 18, the Control Center size slider doesn't resize the Mac Control Center yet.
  • Aerial 5.0 and 5.1 have to be activated in their own settings before they open windows.
  • With Aerial 5.0 or 5.1, don't respring while a VPN is on or was just turned off: Aerial goes online as SpringBoard starts and can hang on a black screen until you restart the iPad. MacStatusBar&Dock warns before resprings that go through iOS.
  • The keys that work inside apps (Shift + = / - for the screen brightness, Tab as Mute) do nothing while a package manager, Filza, iCleaner, TrollStore or the jailbreak app is in front: the tweak leaves those apps alone, so they always open even if something goes wrong.

Both parts switched off? If you turn off both MacStatusBar and MacDock in Settings, their Settings pages go away too. Turn them back on in Choicy (or iCleaner Pro), then respring.

Requirements

  • A hooking platform: ElleKit, libhooker, or Substrate
  • Choicy or the official iCleaner Pro (keeps only one window engine loaded)
  • Optional window engine: Aerial 3.0, 5.0 or 5.1, MilkyWay4 0.1.1 (iPadOS 15 only), Zetsu 1.6.2 or 1.6.6. Other versions run as plain windows, without the Mac look.

1.7.8

New

  • Battery Health on iPad: Settings > Battery has the Battery Health page on every iPad, as on an iPhone (Battery Health & Charging on iPadOS 17 and 18), with the battery's Maximum Capacity and a Cycle Count row. Rows for iPhone-only features, such as Peak Performance Capability, are left out. If another battery health tweak is installed, the page is left to it.
  • Sleep Guard, in a new Battery Use group on the Battery Health page, on by default: when the screen is off and the only thing keeping the iPad awake is a hold left behind by a process that's gone, such as a SpringBoard from before a respring, it clears that hold so the iPad can sleep. It never acts with the screen on or while something else keeps the iPad awake, such as audio, a download or a backup, and at most once in 30 minutes and 6 times a day. iPads stuck awake this way lost about 10 to 28% of their battery a day. While a hold is stuck and not cleared, an iPad Can't Sleep row shows since when.
  • Activity Monitor is in Command-Tab while its window is open, also when it's minimized or on another desktop, with its icon and in the order you last used it. Picking it brings its window forward, and Command-Q closes the window. This doesn't depend on the Show in App Library switch.
  • Swiping past the last Home Screen page no longer opens the App Library. The pages end at the last one, as they do at the first, and this update turns it off on every iPad. To get it back, turn on Swipe Past Last Page Opens App Library: on iPadOS 15 and 16 it's in Apple's Home Screen settings, next to the App Library's rows, and on iPadOS 17 and 18 in the Home Screen group of Settings > Dock. The Dock's Launchpad icon and the App Library's other ways in work as before.

Improvements

  • Less memory: the tweak's parts for apps now load only into apps, their extensions and SpringBoard, no longer into background system services. That saves about 1 MB in each of those services, up to tens of MB in all, depending on the iPad. Services that are already running let go of them when they next start, at the latest when the iPad restarts.
  • Apps use a little less memory too, about 0.2 MB each: the Home Bar part now loads only into SpringBoard, and the audio mixing part adds some of its hooks only when an app first plays sound of its own.
  • iPadOS 18: the tweak no longer lays out its menu bar inside Control Center's hidden status bar, so SpringBoard does about 10 ms less work each time Control Center opens or closes.
  • With the screen off, the tweak's own timers stop: Activity Monitor's refresh (also while its window is minimized), the RAM & CPU tile in Control Center, a Dock check and the screen orientation readings. A 4-second timer in every app now runs only while that app is active. On iPads without Choicy, the tweak's helper no longer looks for Choicy's settings every 5 seconds, day and night.
  • iPadOS 16 to 18: SpringBoard takes a hold that keeps the iPad awake as it starts, and now gives it back a minute later and before a respring from the tweak. A SpringBoard that went away still holding it, for example in an install's respring, could keep the iPad awake for days.
  • Desktop with a trackpad or mouse: a click held still opens the desktop's menu after 0.75 seconds, as iPadOS's own menus do, so a click that pauses before a drag has more time. It was 0.45 seconds. A finger's hold stays as it was, and a two-finger click still opens the menu at once.
  • Add to Folder is now called Add App to Folder, in an app icon's Haptic Touch menu and in its switch in Settings > Status Bar, Add App to Folder in App Menus.

Fixes

  • Mac Switcher with a finger: a swipe up that slows down before you lift goes Home. It could open the Mac Switcher. The Mac Switcher now opens after a short hold, with the finger still for a moment. A trackpad or mouse works as before.
